## Sports Nutrition

The performance on sport the quality of their diet and how much they consume can have a dramatic effect. The amount of energy needed depends on the intensity, duration, frequency, type of exercise, age, gender, level of fitness and body fat stores of the individual. Some tips are:

- Eat a varied and balanced diet
- Eat plenty of carbohydrate rich foods
- Eat at least 5 portions of fruit and veg per day
- Start refuelling with carbohydrate and fluid as soon as possible
- Ensure plenty of liquid is consumed before, during and after exercise

The following nutrients are required during physical activity:

- Carbohydrates
- Protein
- Fat
- Vitamins
- Minerals
- Fluid

### Carbohydrate

A diet rich in carbohydrate is important for the best performance. Complex carbohydrates are best because they are nutrient dense, filling and low in fat. Excess carbohydrate is stored as glycogen.

Maintained glycogen stores are essential for endurance and stamina. Once these stores are used up, muscles cannot work efficiently, and performance is affected. Athletes may become tired, nauseous or dizzy due to dropping sugar levels. It is important to replenish glycogen stores after exercising with low fat, high-carbohydrate snacks.

While most sportspeople maintain a high carbohydrate diet, those involved in endurance event (long distance running) may increase carbohydrate intake by more than 70 per cent in the three days preceding an event. This is known as 'carbohydrate loading' and, combined with a specialist training programme, it can increase glycogen stores and maximise performance.

### Protein

Most sportspeople can meet their protein requirements by following a balanced diet. Main meals should provide some protein, for example, meat, fish, poultry, eggs, dairy, beans, pulses, Quorn and nuts. Only athletes involved in heavy training may need to increase their protein intake in order to gain muscle. This would apply, for example, to weightlifters or those taking part in endurance events, such as marathon runners.

### Fat

A high fat diet makes it difficult to meet carbohydrate requirements and could affect weight management and energy balance. In particular, saturated fat intake should be monitored.

### Fluid Intake

- Fluid intake should always be maintained during strenuous exercise and should be consumed before, during and after the acitivity.
- Water is necessary for proper functioning of the body.
    - Particularly important during summer months.
- Heat is produced as a by-product of work.
    - Must be removed from the body to keep the body at its preferred temperature.
- Sweat is produced to help cool the body.
    - Fluid lost via sweat must be continually replaced to enable this cooling process to continue.

Being well hydrated is essential in order to benefit from training. If sports people approach training and games in a semi-dehydrated condition this will impact on their performance.

Sports drinks have become more popular and can be useful for athletes to help with hydration. The more commonly purchased sports drinks are:

**Isotonic sports drinks**

Isotonic drinks are the **same** concentration as body fluids. These are absorbed as quickly as water is. This type of sports drink is used for fluid replacement by athletes.

Benefits:
- Contain sugar for energy
- Increases rate of absorption

Sports drink should be drinken rarely for people who do not participate in sports since they increase the risk of dental caries and weight gain from the high doses of sugar.

## Food safety

Food safety is about keeping food safe during preparation, cooking and storage, with the aim of preventing the contamination that leads to food poisoning.

### Food poisoning

What causes it?

- Cross contamination - bacteria from raw food being spread to cooked foood indirectly and directly
- Incorrect storage of food - temperature of fridge too high
- Eating food past its use by date
- Insufficient training of staff who handle food
- Not cooking food thoroughly
- Poor personal hygiene e.g. not washing hands before handling food

Foods that are more likely to cause food poisoning than others are called 'high risk':
- Meat
- Poultry
- Pate
- Seafood and shellfish
- cooked rice
- egg based products
- dairy, fresh cream or milk based products

#### E Coli

Symptoms:
- Nausea
- Vomiting
- Diarrhoea (may be bloody)
- Stomach cramps
- Severe anaemia
- Kidney failure

Sources: 
- raw meat
- undercooked mince meat
- gravy
- products made with unpasteurised milk (some cheeses)
- raw fish
- oysters

Method of control:
- prevent cross contamination
- cook meat and meat products thoroughly
- choose pasteurised dairy products


#### Camplyobacter

Symptoms:
- Diarrhoea (may be bloody)
- Stomach cramps
- Fever
- Vomiting

Sources:
- raw or uncooked meat
- poultry
- unpasteurised milk
- untreated water

Method of control:
- prevent cross contamination
- cook meat and poultry thoroughly
- wash prepacked salad leaves

#### Listeria

Symptoms:
- mild flu like symptoms
- nausea
- diarrhoea
- convulsions
- blood poisoning
- meningitis

Sources:
- unpasteurised cheeses
- soft mould ripened cheeses
- uncooked meats
- cold cuts of meat
- smoked salmon
- pates
- ready meals and ready to eat foods

Method of control:
- cook meat and poultry thoroughly
- wash raw veg before eating
- keep uncooked meat separate from all other foods
- choose pasteurised milk and cheese
- prevent cross contamination
- reheat ready made foods until piping hot

#### Staphylococcus aureus

Symptoms:
- severe vomiting
- diarrhoea
- abdominal pain

Sources:
- food made by hand that requires no cooking
- poultry
- cooked meats
- unpasteurised milk

Method of control:
- often lives on hands
- often lives in nose
- high standards of personal hygiene

#### Salmonella

Symptoms:
- Fever
- diarrhoea
- vomiting
- abdominal pain

Sources:
- poultry
- raw meat
- eggs
- unpasteurised milk
- raw unwashed veg

Method of control:
- separate raw food (especially poultry) away from cooked and ready to eat foods
- ensure chicken, eggs and other meats are thoroughly cooked until piping hot in the centre to above 63 degress centigrade
- always wash hands after handling raw chicken and other raw meats
